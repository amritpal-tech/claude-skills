Gainsight cut about 270 roles. The pricing change matters more.

The cuts were reported on November 12, citing a person familiar with the matter. CEO Chuck Ganapathi confirmed them on LinkedIn, without a number.

His line: "This means fewer people in some roles, new skills in others, and roles that didn't exist a year ago."

What Gainsight is changing, per the reports:

↳ From selling customer success software to delivering retention as an outcome.

↳ From seat-based pricing to value-based pricing.

↳ One Outcome Executive accountable for each customer. A person for the largest accounts. An AI agent for the long tail.

Ganapathi says the core business is still highly profitable. So this is a redesign from a position of strength.

Here's the leader read.

When a vendor prices on outcomes, someone has to own the outcome. Gainsight's answer is one owner per customer, and for most customers that owner is an agent.

Three things I'd do before the next renewal of any GTM tool:

1. Ask each vendor which outcome they'd price on, and how they'd measure it.
2. Ask who owns your account if you're in the long tail. A named person or an agent.
3. Write the outcome definition into the contract before you agree to the price.

Here's the honest part: the 270 figure comes from an anonymous source, and there are no published results from this model yet.

Automate the typing. Never automate the judgment.

Outcome pricing needs an outcome owner.

Would you accept an AI agent as your account owner if the price followed the results?
