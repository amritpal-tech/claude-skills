An AI-native firm is six layers and one rule.

In March, Sequoia's Julien Bek argued that the next $1T company will sell finished work, with AI doing much of it and people bringing the expertise.

His essay sorts services by how much judgment the work takes.

It's already happening. On November 12, Morningscore, an SEO software company, started selling a done-for-you service. It puts the mix at about 70% automation and 30% human.

Here's how I'd lay out the firm. Agent work first, the human part second.

1. Find.
Agent: builds and scores the account list, and watches for signals.
Human: decides who the firm is for.

2. Warm.
Agent: drafts the newsletter, the follow-ups and the webinar recap.
Human: brings the point of view nobody else has.

3. Close.
Agent: researches the account, takes the call notes, updates the CRM.
Human: runs the call and sets the price.

4. Start.
Agent: contract, invoice, kickoff checklist.
Human: makes the first promise to the client.

5. Deliver.
Agent: runs the productized service from written SOPs.
Human: reviews every output before it ships.

6. Remember.
Agent: files every call, lead, deliverable and SOP in one searchable place.
Human: decides what the firm learned.

The rule under all six: automate the typing, never automate the judgment.

Here's the honest part: Morningscore's 70/30 is its own estimate, and the software-margin promise is still a thesis.

Save this before you price your first productized service.
