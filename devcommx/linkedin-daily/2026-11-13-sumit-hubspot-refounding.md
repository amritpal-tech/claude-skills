HubSpot changed its CEO. Its buyers changed first.

On November 12, co-founder Brian Halligan returned as HubSpot's CEO, effective immediately.

Yamini Rangan, CEO since 2021, stepped down.

Halligan says he and Dharmesh Shah are "re-founding" the company.

The release frames the next chapter as "building the customer relationship harness that will enable millions to grow better."

That's the headline. The context sits in HubSpot's Q3, a week earlier.

↳ Revenue was $947.6M, up 17%, with more than 311,000 customers.

↳ Rangan said the headwinds from Q2 "continued and became more pronounced in Q3."

↳ Larger customers had longer approval cycles and bigger buying committees. Smaller ones got more price sensitive.

↳ More buyers want to test AI products on their own data before they commit.

The realist read for anyone selling AI:

If a company with 311,000 customers feels this, check your own pipeline for it.

Three things I'd change in an AI sales motion this quarter:

1. Offer a trial on the buyer's own data, with the success measure agreed before it starts.
2. Find out early who else signs off. Security, IT, finance.
3. Give AI deals a longer cycle in the forecast until the proof lands.

If you run on HubSpot, nothing changes on Monday.

Halligan says he'll share why he came back and what he plans in the next few days. Read that before you touch your stack.

One honest disqualifier: nobody outside HubSpot knows yet what the "harness" means in product terms.

Buyers want proof before they want features.

Is your AI deal cycle getting longer this quarter?
