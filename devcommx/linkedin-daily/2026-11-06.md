### Post
<!-- post:amritpal format:single-image ask:sharp-question type:news -->
Opting out of AI training could cost you Gemini citations.

On October 30, Google, Microsoft, OpenAI and Anthropic backed one shared robots.txt signal for AI training. The token is __usage_ai-training.

It's a good change. It also hides a trap for anyone who wants to be cited.

Per coverage of Google's plans, if you opt out with the shared token and don't set Google-Extended separately, Google will read it as a Google-Extended block. That covers Gemini grounding as well as training.

So a site that only meant "don't train on us" could drop out of Gemini's answers.

Microsoft splits the two: training preferences in robots.txt, grounding on its own control. It says neither one removes a page from Bing search by itself.

Here's the audit I'd run before any of this switches on in 2027:

1. Read your robots.txt today. Check whether someone already blocked AI bots wholesale.
2. Decide training and citations separately. They're two decisions now.
3. If Gemini citations matter, set Google-Extended explicitly, after reading Google's own documentation.
4. Remember that "User-agent: *" doesn't cover the new token. You have to name it.
5. Put February 1, 2027 in the calendar. That's when Microsoft starts honouring the preference.

One honest limit: none of the four has switched this on yet, and the details vary by source. Check each company's documentation before you ship a change.

Being cited is a permission you can revoke by accident.

Does your robots.txt still let the AI that would cite you read your site?

### Post
<!-- post:pankaj format:text ask:sharp-question type:news -->
47% of RevOps teams can't fully change a pricing rule.

That's from the RevOps AI Authority Benchmark, published October 28 by RevOps Co-op and DealHub. 48 of 102 respondents said they can't fully change quoting or pricing rules without IT or engineering.

18% can't change them at all.

Now add agents that quote.

If a rule lives in code only engineering can touch, every pricing change becomes a ticket. Every agent that quotes inherits the wait.

Here's how I'd wire it before an agent touches a quote:

1. Inventory every pricing and quoting rule, and where it lives: CPQ config, code, or a spreadsheet someone emails around.
2. Move the rules into config RevOps owns, with a change log and a named approver.
3. Test every change against a fixed set of sample quotes before it ships.
4. Agents read rules. They never write them, and anything outside a discount threshold goes to a human.
5. Keep a kill switch that drops agents back to draft-only.

Honest limit: 102 respondents, and DealHub sells CPQ. Treat the number as a signal and check your own team before you trust it.

If RevOps can't change a rule, an agent shouldn't be the one applying it.

Which pricing rule in your stack would take longest to change today?
