## Post
<!-- post:sumit format:single-image ask:sharp-question type:evergreen -->
Nine agents, one job each. Three places a human signs.

That's how I'd staff outbound with AI today.

One agent doing everything writes generic email. One agent per job can be checked, swapped and fixed.

The team:

1. ICP picker. Ranks two or three ICPs by fit and past wins.
2. Signal watcher. Funding, hiring, leadership and tool changes, each with a date.
3. Researcher. Reads the site, customer stories and review pages, then scores fit. Only the top scores move on.
4. Buyer mapper. Finds the people who sign, use and block.
5. First-touch writer. Starts from a campaign angle. No meeting ask in email one.
6. Follow-up writer. A new reason at every step.
7. Reply sorter. Tags each reply as interested, not now or no, and drafts a response.
8. CRM keeper. Merges duplicate contacts and companies, so no one hears from you twice.
9. Closed-lost reviver. Re-reads lost deals for what changed since.

The three signatures:

↳ A person picks the ICP.

↳ A person approves the angle before the first send.

↳ A person answers every interested reply.

If I could hire only one, it would be number 9. The Claude agent that re-read our own HubSpot closed-lost surfaced ~$380K of written-off pipeline.

Run the team on Grok, Claude or GPT. The model matters less than the handoffs: one input, one output, one owner per agent.

Agents do the typing. People keep the judgment.

Which of the nine would you hire first?
