Your robots.txt says yes. Your server may say no.

The November AI Access Index tested 684 of the top 1,000 hostnames on the web.

It read each site's robots.txt, then requested the homepage as GPTBot, OAI-SearchBot, ClaudeBot and PerplexityBot, and kept the status code.

Of the 547 sites whose robots.txt allows at least one of those crawlers, 62 turned at least one of them away. That's 11.3%.

It runs the other way too. Of the 124 sites that block one in robots.txt, 79 served it anyway.

Here's the actual mechanism.

Robots.txt is a request. The firewall, the CDN and the bot settings decide what a crawler really gets.

If they disagree with robots.txt, the AI search crawler you want can't read the page you wrote for it.

The checks I'd run:

1. List the AI crawlers your robots.txt allows, and why: training, search or both.
2. Request your homepage with each crawler's user agent and log the status code. A 403, 429 or 503 is a no.
3. Open your CDN's bot settings and compare them with robots.txt, line by line.
4. Search your server logs for those user agents and the status codes they got last month.
5. Name one owner for the AI crawler policy, so marketing and IT stop setting it separately.

One honest limit: a request with a borrowed user agent isn't the real crawler. A CDN that checks crawler IPs can treat the two differently, so read its logs too.

AI visibility starts with a status code.

Save this before your next technical SEO review.

<!-- post:pankaj format:single-image ask:save-this type:news -->
Your client can now pay your tool's Claude bill.

On November 17, Anthropic launched linked billing in beta. In its words: "Your customers can now pay for the Claude usage in your app through their own Claude organization."

An admin on the client's side approves the link and sets a monthly spend limit. Either side can unlink at any time.

For anyone building Claude-powered GTM tools for clients, that changes the margin math. It also adds plumbing.

Who pays is decided per request, with a billing policy that bills the client or bills you.

Five things to wire before linking a client:

1. A table that maps each client to their linked profile, owned like a CRM owner field. A wrong mapping bills the wrong client.
2. A route for what can't be billed to the client. Message Batches and billable code execution stay on your own bill.
3. An eligibility check. The client's organization must pay Anthropic directly for API usage. Monthly API credits from Max, Ultra and Team plans can't pay.
4. A compliance screen. The requests run in your organization, so the client's ZDR, BAA and CMEK don't apply. Anthropic says not to link clients that need HIPAA, FERPA or CMEK coverage.
5. An alert for spend-limit refusals, so a capped client gets a clear message instead of a broken workflow.

It's a beta, and Anthropic says endpoints and behaviour can change.

The billing field decides who gets the invoice. Test it like one.

Save this before you link your first client.
