## Research brief
- **The point (Snowflake's Expedition 2026 recap for sales leaders):** Florian Delval, Principal Product Marketing for AI and Marketing at Snowflake, argues that AI agents expose inconsistent pipeline and attribution definitions. Marketing, sales and RevOps often use the same words to mean different things, and teams live with it until agents start answering from those inconsistent definitions. The recap says the block is ownership, definitions and workflows more than models.
- **Snowflake's product answer:** Business Ontology, which lets a company approve what a term such as "net revenue" means, assign an owner, and link the definition to the relevant data; agents then use the approved definitions.
- **Independent caveat (MarketScale):** loading candidate definitions is easy; deciding who signs off on what a term means is the harder, human part that no software release handles.

- **Why it matters to the reader:** CROs are putting agents on top of pipeline and attribution data. If "pipeline" or "qualified" means three things, the agent will confidently give three answers.
- **Angle:** the definition is a leadership decision. Settle three of them, give each an owner, before any agent answers a revenue question.

## Post
<!-- post:sumit format:text ask:repost-if type:news -->
Marketing: Pipeline is up.

Sales: Which pipeline?

That argument used to cost a meeting. With AI agents, it costs every answer.

Snowflake's recap of its Expedition event puts it well. Marketing, sales and RevOps often use the same words to mean different things, and teams live with it until agents start answering from those inconsistent definitions.

Snowflake's own fix is a product: a Business Ontology where a company approves what a term like "net revenue" means, gives it an owner and links it to the data agents use.

Here's the leader read.

The software is the easy part. Deciding who signs off on what "pipeline" means is a leadership call, and no release makes it for you.

Three definitions I'd settle before any agent answers a revenue question:

1. Pipeline. Which stages count, from which date, in whose numbers.
2. Qualified. One meaning, written down, used by marketing and sales alike.
3. Attribution. Which touch gets the credit, and who can change the rule.

Give each one an owner and a date it was last reviewed.

One honest disqualifier: this comes from a vendor that sells the fix. The problem it names is easy to check in your own CRM this week.

Automate the typing. Never automate the judgment.

An agent is only as consistent as your definitions.

Repost if your marketing and sales teams report different pipeline numbers.
