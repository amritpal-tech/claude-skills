You used to brief Claude step by step. Fable 5.2 asks for the finish line.

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 on November 9, and it just showed up in my plan.

The launch screen sells it in three lines:

→ Bring complex ideas to life with animations, simulations and 3D worlds.

→ Describe the result you want, and it figures out how to get there.

→ Get long, multi-step projects done with fewer check-ins.

The numbers behind it, from Anthropic's model page:

↳ AutomationBench, which tests business workflows: 51.4%, up from 31.4% for Fable 5.1.

↳ API price: $8 per million input tokens and $40 output, down from $10 and $50.

↳ On my plan, it's included for up to 50% of weekly usage.

Here's the actual mechanism. When the model plans its own steps, your brief becomes the product. A vague goal now gets a confident, vague result, faster.

Here's how I'd test it on marketing work this week:

1. Brief the outcome. "A pricing-page FAQ answering the 10 questions buyers ask sales, two lines each, with sources" beats "write an FAQ".
2. Hand it one long job. A content audit across 50 posts, or a page cluster rewritten so every section answers one question.
3. Build one interactive asset. An ROI calculator or a product walkthrough simulation that would normally wait in a developer's queue.
4. Ration it. Anthropic itself recommends Opus 5.5 for most everyday work, so keep Fable for the planning-heavy jobs.

One honest disqualifier: on that same business-workflow benchmark, Gemini 4 Carbon still scores higher, at 53.9%. Test it on your own work before you move a workflow.

The better the model plans, the more your brief is worth.
