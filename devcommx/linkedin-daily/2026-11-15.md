### Research brief
- **What happened:** during the Microsoft 365 outage that began shortly before 1 p.m. ET on Thursday, Nov 12, Microsoft extended its email timeout window to 48 hours "to help preserve delayed messages while it works through email queues and backlogs" (normally Exchange Online keeps retrying for 24 hours before bouncing). Messages sent to Microsoft 365 addresses during the outage sat in queues, at the sending platform or at Microsoft, and were delivered as recovery continued. Microsoft traced the outage to a flawed network capacity upgrade. Incident IDs: EX1550543 (Exchange Online) and MO1550821. Sources disagree on the end time (16:10 UTC Nov 13 per one; recovery into Nov 14 per another).
- **Sources:** [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/a-huge-pain-in-the-ass-microsoft-365-starts-to-recover-after-a-daylong-global-email-blackout/) · [BetaNews](https://betanews.com/article/microsoft-365-outage-network-upgrade/) · [Postbox](https://postboxservices.com/industry-updates/microsoft-365-exchange-online-outage-november-2026/) · [Enrow](https://enrow.io/en/blog/microsoft-outlook-outage-november-2026) (via search summaries)
- **Mechanism (general, not from the sources):** Microsoft 365 domains publish MX records ending in `mail.protection.outlook.com`, so a sending stack can tell which recipients sit on Microsoft.
- **Why it matters to the reader:** outbound tools and list-hygiene rules treat bounces as a signal about the address. During an outage a bounce is a signal about the provider. Sequences also kept running while earlier steps sat in queues.
- **Angle:** replay the outage window before it pollutes your data: suppression list, step order, reply-rate tests, and add the field that lets you pause one mail provider next time.

<!-- post:pankaj format:single-image ask:save-this type:news -->
Some of last Thursday's bounces weren't bad addresses.

During the Microsoft 365 outage, Microsoft extended its email timeout window to 48 hours to keep delayed messages from expiring.

Normally Exchange Online keeps retrying for 24 hours before it bounces.

Mail sent to Microsoft 365 addresses sat in queues, at your sending platform or at Microsoft, and landed as recovery went on.

Microsoft has since traced the outage to a network capacity upgrade.

So for about a day, a bounce said more about Microsoft than about the address.

Your stack doesn't know that. Hygiene rules suppress, sequences advance, dashboards count.

The outage replay I'd run this week:

1. Pull every send to domains whose MX ends in mail.protection.outlook.com, from Thursday 1 p.m. ET until your queues cleared.
2. Check the suppression list for addresses added in that window. Restore the ones that bounced only then.
3. Look for step two landing before step one. Reply logic that keys on step order will misfire.
4. Tag the window as an outage in reporting, so reply rates and A/B tests leave it out.
5. Add a mail-host field to every account, filled from the MX lookup. Next time, you pause one provider in one click.

The fix is one field.

Your data is only as clean as your worst day of sending.

Save this for the next outage.
