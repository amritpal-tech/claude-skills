**Slot:** off today · for **Fri 4 Dec · 14:00 IST** (09:30 CET) · **Pillar:** AI search & LLM visibility · **Archetype:** system-as-content · **Rung:** 2 (Nov 20 report) with rung 4 evidence (studies collected in a mid-November meta-analysis)

### Research brief
- **What happened (Nov 20):** Search Engine Land reported that Google says AI search creates more opportunities for brands to be discovered, and that SEOs and site owners are pushing back over declining clicks and limited reporting. Google's Search Central docs say there are no additional requirements or special optimizations to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode.
- **The evidence on clicks:** Search Engine Land's Q4 2026 meta-analysis collects the controlled studies without pooling them. Wang et al. (field experiment, 1,100 active US participants): routing searches into AI Mode cut external click-through by 18.8 percentage points vs normal Google search; the group with AI features hidden clicked 8.8 points more, with no measurable change in satisfaction. Agarwal and Sen (field experiment): when an AI Overview appeared, outbound organic clicks fell 39.8%.
- **Caveats in the sources:** both are working papers. The Wang study ran for a week; AI Mode was 0.6% of participants' searches before the test, so it measures a forced switch; the sample skewed younger and more educated.
- **What the sources do not say (as summarised):** Google's exact wording, or whether its article came with new reporting. The post attributes Google's claim to Search Engine Land's report and quotes no Google wording.
- **Sources:** [Search Engine Land, Nov 20](https://searchengineland.com/google-ai-search-advice-seo-backlash-502610) · [Search Engine Land, Q4 2026 meta-analysis](https://searchengineland.com/ai-search-meta-analysis-q4-2026-502232) · [Google: AI features and your website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) (all blocked here; via search summaries)
- **Why it matters to the reader:** marketing leaders are asked why organic traffic is down while Google says AI search helps. Both can hold. The report has to show where the brand was seen, not just who clicked.
- **Angle:** Google and the studies measure different things. Add presence, branded demand and self-reported source to the monthly report, and read clicks by page type.

<!-- post:amritpal format:single-image ask:sharp-question type:news -->
Google and the click studies measure different things.

Google says AI search gives brands more chances to be discovered. That's from Search Engine Land's report on November 20, which also covers the pushback from SEOs over falling clicks and thin reporting.

The controlled studies looked at clicks.

In one field experiment with 1,100 US participants, sending searches into AI Mode cut clicks to outside websites by 18.8 percentage points. Hiding the AI features raised clicks by 8.8 points, with no measurable change in satisfaction.

In another, outbound organic clicks fell 39.8% when an AI Overview appeared.

Here's how I read both.

Discovery happens inside the answer now. The click is optional.

So a report built only on organic clicks will show decline, even in a month when more buyers saw your name.

What I'd add to the monthly report:

1. Presence: how often you're named for your top 20 buying questions, in AI Mode and AI Overviews.
2. Branded search demand, tracked apart from non-branded.
3. A source question on the demo form, with "an AI assistant" as one of the answers.
4. Clicks by page type, so you see which pages still earn the visit.

Two caveats: both studies are working papers. The AI Mode one ran for a week and pushed people into a mode that was 0.6% of their searches before.

Clicks tell you what happened after the answer. Presence tells you what happened in it.

When your leadership team asks about search, which number do they ask for first: clicks or mentions?

Reply's open-source SDR keeps its history on your machine.

Jason, Reply's open-source AI SDR, is MIT-licensed and on GitHub. Version 0.49.1 shipped on November 20, after several releases a day that week.

It runs inside Claude Code, the host Reply says it verifies against. Codex, Cursor CLI, Gemini CLI and others are listed, most not yet run on a real account.

The README is clear about where the state lives: "A durable runtime on your machine holds campaigns, per-person plans, approvals, questions and an append-only history."

That's the line RevOps should stop on.

Your outbound history sits on one person's machine, outside the CRM, until you wire it back.

Five calls to make before a real campaign:

1. A test sending account. The README says to approve real sends only in one you keep for testing.
2. One pinned release per campaign. With several releases a day, a mid-campaign update is a change nobody reviewed.
3. A path for sends, replies and meetings into the CRM. If nothing writes them back, your pipeline reports can't see the campaign.
4. The approval gate, left on. Every operation that reaches a real person waits for approval until you change it.
5. Your own contacts and warm-up. Jason has neither.

The agent can be free. The system of record still has to be the CRM.

Repost if your RevOps team wants every send logged where the pipeline lives.
