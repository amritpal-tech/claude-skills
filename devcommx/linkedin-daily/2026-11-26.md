ChatGPT ads now show how many people saw them.

OpenAI's advertiser update on November 25 added nine reach and frequency metrics. Until now, reports showed impressions served, with no count of how many people saw an ad or how often.

The new ones include Reach (unique people who saw your ads at least once in the date range), Total Frequency, Unique CTR, Unique CPC and Cost per 1,000 Reached.

The same update added a "Learn more" button on single image ads and clearer rejection messages. Sponsored Business Agents are rolling out to eligible businesses outside the US.

Here's why reach matters for B2B.

A buying group is a handful of people. Impressions can't tell you whether you reached them five times or reached fifty strangers once.

Reach and frequency can. It's the same pair you already plan LinkedIn against.

If you're testing ChatGPT ads, I'd set it up like this:

1. Report reach and frequency next to LinkedIn's, for the same weeks.
2. Set a frequency ceiling before launch, and pause when you pass it.
3. Judge Unique CTR. One curious person clicking five times is still one person.
4. Compare Cost per 1,000 Reached with your LinkedIn cost per 1,000 for a similar audience.
5. Keep the test small until conversions are tracked end to end.

The honest limit: reach is ChatGPT's own count on ChatGPT's own surface. It can't tell you who inside a buying group saw the ad.

A channel you can't count people on is a guess. This one just became countable.

Is ChatGPT in your 2027 paid test budget yet, or still on the watch list?

The catch: anyone can claim to be an agent in Cequence's data, and bots posing as agents grew 27-fold over the same months. Some "buyer agents" won't be buyers.

So I'd write the policy now. Accept agent-booked meetings, confirm a human before the call, and keep anything sensitive for the person.

Write the rule before the first one lands.

Would your team take a demo booked by a buyer's agent?

Workflows are getting a review step in n8n.

Version 2.50.0, a pre-release from November 24, adds workflow reviews and tighter promotion between environments.

From the changelog:

"Enforce workflow reviews on instance level"

"Allow users to close a workflow review without approval"

"Show the promotion environment of the project in the change preview"

"Stop a Promote when the project changed after the preview"

In plain terms: an admin can require a review before a change goes live, and a promotion stops if the project moved after you previewed it.

The workflows that route leads and write CRM fields deserve the same review as code.

How I'd set it up once it's stable:

1. Split dev and production projects, with source control on both.
2. Require reviews for every workflow that writes to the CRM or sends email.
3. Name a reviewer for each one, someone other than the builder.
4. Preview before every promote. If the promote stops, re-preview. Don't force it.
5. Log who approved each promotion, so the record outlives the reviewer.

Version 2.49.6 became the stable build on November 25. Test 2.50 on a staging instance first, and check the defaults it changes, like durable poll triggers.

A routing change nobody reviewed is a pipeline change nobody approved.

Repost if your lead routing still changes without a second pair of eyes.
