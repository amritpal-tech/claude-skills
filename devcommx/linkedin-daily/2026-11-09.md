<!-- post:amritpal format:carousel ask:save-this type:news -->
Followers gained used to be a number. Now it's people.

LinkedIn has shown a followers-gained count per post since 2025. On November 3, Sam Corrao Clanon, who leads creator product at LinkedIn, announced the next step: a Post Performance Table. It's rolling out globally on desktop first, with mobile to follow.

It puts every post in one view you can export: impressions, reach, video views, watch time, followers gained, reactions, comments, reposts, saves and sends.

Open the followers-gained number and you see the members who followed from that post.

Here's the actual mechanism. Impressions tell you a post travelled. Followers tell you it made someone want the next one.

For a B2B team, the second signal sits much closer to pipeline.

Here's how I'd use it for an exec or team posting program:

1. Export the table monthly for everyone who posts.
2. Rank posts by followers gained per 1,000 impressions. Raw followers reward reach. The ratio rewards the topic.
3. Open the top five and look at who followed. Titles, companies, seniority. Do they match the accounts you sell to?
4. Write more of what pulls the right people, and less of what only pulls reach.
5. Keep every export. Six months of them is your best evidence of what your buyers want to read.

One honest disqualifier: it's desktop first and still rolling out. If you can't see it yet, check again later this month.

Reach tells you who saw it. Followers tell you who wanted more.

Save this for your next content review.
