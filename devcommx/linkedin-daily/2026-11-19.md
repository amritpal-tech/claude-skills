Google just gave AI Mode an incognito button.

On November 18, Google rolled out temporary threads in AI Mode, worldwide. You start one from the icon in the top-right corner.

Inside a temporary thread, answers aren't shaped by your past preferences and don't feed future personalization. The chat stays out of your search history.

Here's why it matters for AEO.

The people who check a brand's AI visibility are usually the people who search that brand all day. Their history can tilt what AI Mode shows them.

A temporary thread takes that history out of the test.

What I'd change in your checks:

1. Run every tracked AI Mode prompt in a temporary thread from now on.
2. Run a handful signed in as well, and log where the two answers differ.
3. Keep the wording, language and region the same each time, so a change means something.
4. Log the date of every run. The AI Mode header stopped naming the model.
5. Note who gets cited and who gets linked, in separate columns.

One honest disqualifier: a temporary thread removes your past preferences. It doesn't show you what a buyer in another city or language would see.

A clean test beats a friendly one.

Save this before your next AI visibility audit.

<!-- post:pankaj format:text ask:save-this type:news -->
Two more assistants just got write access to your CRM.

On November 17, OpenAI launched ChatGPT for Sales. Per the launch coverage, its Salesforce plugin can read and write records. HubSpot, Close and monday CRM are among its 30-plus partner plugins.

The same day, Microsoft and Salesforce put a Salesforce plugin into Copilot, in public preview. It works under each seller's existing Salesforce permissions.

That last line is the one to wire around.

When an assistant acts with the seller's permissions, every edit right your reps have becomes an assistant edit right.

What I'd do before switching either one on:

1. Pull the profiles and permission sets your sellers use. List the objects and fields they can edit.
2. Lock the fields an assistant shouldn't touch: amount, close date, stage, owner. Field-level security or validation rules.
3. Start read-only for one team, for two weeks.
4. Check how assistant writes show up in field history: as the seller, or as an integration user. If it's the seller, add a source field.
5. Review the change log weekly until you trust it.

The part nobody budgets for is the audit trail.

An assistant inherits every permission you never cleaned up.

Save this before your admin clicks enable.
