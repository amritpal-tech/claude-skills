Agents that work for days need managers, too.

Anthropic shipped Fable 5.2 on Monday.

Read the model page as a GTM leader and one line jumps out.

Anthropic says it can run ambiguous engineering work for days and coordinate hundreds, even more than a thousand, subagents.

The app screen puts it in plainer words: fewer check-ins on long projects.

Fewer check-ins is the feature.

It's also where the risk lives.

What else changed:

↳ Business workflows: 31.4% to 51.4% on AutomationBench, a 20-point jump from Fable 5.1. Gemini 4 Carbon still edges it at 53.9%.

↳ The API got 20% cheaper: $8 in, $40 out, per million tokens.

↳ It comes with a cap. The launch screen in the image allows it for half your weekly usage at most, and Anthropic still points most everyday work to Opus 5.5.

The realist read for anyone running a GTM team:

1. Budget per job. A cheaper token on a job that runs for days still adds up.
2. Put the check-ins where the money is. Pricing, contracts, anything a customer will see.
3. Give the long jobs to ops. One team learns to brief it well, and everyone else uses the output.

Automate the typing. Never automate the judgment.

A model that needs fewer check-ins needs better ones.

Which long GTM job would you hand it first, and where would you put the check-in?
