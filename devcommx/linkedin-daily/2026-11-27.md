Google just wrote down how AI Overviews pick their links.

On November 24, Google added a page to Search Central on attribution and factuality in its generative AI features. The page applies to AI Mode as well as AI Overviews.

In short: the systems pull results from the Search index, the model writes the answer from the pages it found for grounding, and then it cites and links the pages that support what it says.

Which links make the cut is a balance. Google weighs relevance and quality signals from Search ranking against a clear layout on desktop and mobile and a diverse set of helpful sources.

What that means in practice:

Three things decide whether you're linked. The page is indexed. It's eligible to show a snippet. And it supports a specific claim in the answer.

Five checks on your top pages:

1. Indexed: confirm it in Search Console's page indexing report.
2. Snippet-eligible: look for nosnippet, max-snippet:0 or data-nosnippet on the parts you want quoted.
3. One claim, one paragraph: put the fact and its evidence together, so a sentence can support an answer on its own.
4. A distinct angle: if three pages already say the same thing, a push for diverse sources favours the one that adds something.
5. Ask the question in AI Mode and log which page gets linked, yours or someone else's.

The honest limit: the page describes the process, not the weights. Nobody outside Google knows how much each signal counts.

Ranking gets you considered. Supporting the claim gets you cited.

Save this before your next content refresh.

<!-- post:pankaj format:single-image ask:save-this type:evergreen -->
HubSpot gives you 10 product usage events. Pick carefully.

HubSpot's customer success workspace tracks activation and product usage, built on custom events.

Activation is a one-time event: the customer started using a product or feature. Usage is ongoing: how often they engage. Add a frequency expectation, and CSMs see adoption and drops in one place.

The limit that shapes everything: 10 activation and usage events per portal.

The data has to get there first. HubSpot's native behavioral events track website interactions. Product events from your app usually come in through the Events API, reverse ETL or a CDP.

The build, in five steps:

1. List the product actions that come before a renewal or an expansion. Keep the ones that predict it, drop the vanity ones.
2. Define one activation event per product, written down with its exact trigger.
3. Send product events through the Events API with stable names. Renaming an event later breaks the history.
4. Set each frequency expectation from what healthy accounts actually do over 30 days.
5. Turn a missed expectation into a CSM task, with the account owner copied.

The part teams skip: a usage event that doesn't map to renewal is noise with a dashboard.

Ten slots means every event is a decision.

Save this before you wire product usage into HubSpot.

4. Expect employees to use AI to challenge decisions. Keep the reasoning behind people decisions in writing.

The honest limit: the survey covers employers broadly, and "actual or anticipated" AI use mixes what happened with what's expected.

Accountability has to move as fast as the automation.

Repost if your AI policy still doesn't have a name next to it.
