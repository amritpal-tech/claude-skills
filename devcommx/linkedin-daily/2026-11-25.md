13% of AI agent visits now go past browsing.

Cequence's Agentic AI Security Index, out November 23, tracked AI agent traffic from May 16 to November 15.

The share of agent visits that signed in, booked, bought or managed an account rose from 1.4% to 13%.

Over the same months, bots posing as AI agents and probing for secrets grew 27-fold. They went after config files, passwords, keys and admin consoles.

Two caveats from the report itself. Agent names are self-reported, so anyone can claim one. And agents are still under 0.1% of all requests.

Small, but it lands on your forms, your booking page and your login.

Five things to wire now:

1. Capture the user agent on every form submit and booking, and write it to a "submitted via" field in the CRM.
2. Treat the agent name as a claim. Check it against the vendor's published IP ranges or request signatures where they exist.
3. Route agent-made bookings through a quick human check before they take a slot on a rep's calendar.
4. Alert on agent-named traffic that hits admin paths, config files or login pages. That's the impostor pattern.
5. Report the agent share monthly, next to bot and human traffic.

The trap: block every agent and you may lose a real buyer who sent one.

Every lead needs a source of truth for who submitted it: a person, an agent or a bot.

How would your CRM know today whether a person or an agent booked the meeting?

### Research brief
- **The rule:** under Article 50 of the EU AI Act, providers of AI systems that generate synthetic audio, image, video or text must mark the output in a machine-readable way so it can be detected as AI-generated (Article 50(2)). Systems already on the market before August 2, 2026 have until **December 2, 2026** to implement the marking (Morgan Lewis; Jones Walker). Morgan Lewis adds that this transition does not postpone deployer duties.
- **Already in force:** the duty to inform people when they're interacting with an AI system applied from August 2, 2026, and the Digital Omnibus did not delay it (Gibson Dunn: it "remains unaffected and proceeds as scheduled"). The omnibus moved Annex III high-risk obligations to December 2, 2027 and Annex I (embedded AI) to August 2, 2028.
- **Guidance:** the Commission adopted Article 50 guidelines on July 20, 2026; a voluntary code of practice on marking AI-generated content was published June 10, 2026.
- **What the sources don't settle:** whether AI that drafts and sends email under a rep's name counts as "interacting directly" with a person. The post names it as a grey area for counsel.
- **Sources:** [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/blogs/sourcingatmorganlewis/2026/08/eu-ai-acts-transparency-rules-what-went-into-effect-on-2-august) · [Jones Walker](https://www.joneswalker.com/en/insights/blogs/ai-law-blog/yes-august-2-still-matters-the-eu-approved-a-high-risk-ai-delay-but-most-trans.html) · [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/) (via search summaries)

December 2 is the next EU AI Act deadline.

It concerns AI that generates content. Under Article 50, providers of systems that produce synthetic audio, images, video or text must mark that output in a machine-readable way. Systems already on the market before August 2 have until December 2 to comply.

The other Article 50 duty is already in force. Since August 2, people must be told when they're interacting with an AI system.

The Digital Omnibus pushed the high-risk rules to 2027 and 2028. It left this one where it was.

For revenue teams selling in Europe, that lands closer than it looks: chat agents on the website, voice agents, AI-written outreach, generated images and video in campaigns.

The decision underneath it is ownership. Someone has to know every place a buyer meets AI, and what each one discloses.

Four steps I'd put on the leadership agenda before Wednesday:

1. List every point where a buyer or customer meets AI: chat, voice, email, content.
2. Check that each conversational agent says it's AI, inside the conversation itself.
3. Ask each vendor, in writing, how its tool meets the December 2 marking requirement.
4. Take the grey areas to counsel. AI that drafts and sends email under a rep's name is the obvious one.

The omnibus bought time on the high-risk rules. The transparency rules are due now.

Which of your customer-facing AI tools could your team not list from memory today?
