<!-- post:pankaj format:text ask:sharp-question type:evergreen -->
The GTM stack, in 12 eras.

Each one moved the data somewhere new, and broke something on the way.

1. The Rolodex (1950s)
Gave: contacts at your fingertips.
Broke: they left when the rep did.

2. The spreadsheet (1979, VisiCalc)
Gave: numbers anyone could model.
Broke: forty versions of the forecast.

3. The contact manager (1987, ACT!)
Gave: accounts on your own PC.
Broke: stuck on your own PC.

4. Client-server CRM (1993, Siebel)
Gave: one shared customer database.
Broke: IT owned it, sales avoided it.

5. Cloud CRM (1999, Salesforce)
Gave: the CRM in a browser.
Broke: fields that multiply forever.

6. Marketing automation (2006, HubSpot and Marketo)
Gave: lead scores.
Broke: the MQL argument.

7. Integration glue (2011, Zapier)
Gave: anyone could connect two apps.
Broke: nobody knows what syncs where.

8. Sales engagement (2011, Salesloft)
Gave: sequences at scale.
Broke: activity lives in a second system.

9. Contact databases (2010s)
Gave: an email for anyone.
Broke: bounce rates and decay.

10. Reverse ETL (around 2020)
Gave: warehouse data back in the tools.
Broke: three systems claiming to be the master.

11. Waterfall enrichment (early 2020s)
Gave: fill rates from many providers.
Broke: nobody knows which provider wrote the field.

12. Agents over MCP (2024 onward)
Gave: AI that reads and writes the CRM.
Broke: every agent is a new user with write access.

Every era bought speed and paid in data quality.

The tools keep changing. The field definitions stay your job.

Which era's mess are you still cleaning up?
