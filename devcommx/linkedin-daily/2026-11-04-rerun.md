### Research brief
- **What happened:** OpenAI's consumer product lead Adam Fry told Alex Kantrowitz (Big Technology), at an OpenAI dinner in New York on Monday Nov 2, that ChatGPT's search volume is "roughly one-fifth (20%) of the volume of queries of Google." He also said ChatGPT grew from 1 billion to 1.2 billion weekly active users in the last two months. Reported Nov 3.
- **How OpenAI counts:** a search is counted whenever someone prompts ChatGPT to search the web; Google volume counts queries submitted to a results page. OpenAI has not published the data. Gizmodo: "search-like queries" is an eye-of-the-beholder thing.
- **Second data point:** Bluefish says Google and ChatGPT name different leading brands in 35% of 5,810 buying topics across three categories. Method not disclosed. BrightEdge found similar divergence about a year earlier.
- **Sources:** [9to5Google, Nov 3](https://9to5google.com/2026/11/03/chatgpt-has-hit-roughly-20-of-google-search-volume-openai-says/) · [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/chatgpt-claims-to-have-achieved-20-of-googles-search-volume-2000834464) · [ppc.land on Bluefish](https://ppc.land/chatgpt-and-google-differ-on-top-brand-in-35-of-buying-topics-bluefish-says/)
- **Why it matters to his reader:** a B2B marketing scoreboard built on Google rank plus one blended "AI visibility" number cannot see an engine that disagrees with Google a third of the time.
- **What the sources do not say:** anything B2B-specific, any per-category split of the 20%, or Bluefish's method. The post says so.
- **Angle:** the claim is soft; the measurement change is not. Measure per engine.

### Post
<!-- post:pankaj format:text ask:sharp-question type:news -->
Your ad audiences are now a sync job.

Clay's changelog describes Ads 2.0 as always-on audiences that refresh themselves.

Destinations: LinkedIn, Meta and Google, plus newly added Bing, Vibe.co and Reddit.

Suppression lists stay on too. They re-sync as accounts and contacts qualify in or out, and EU opt-outs update across every synced platform.

That's useful. It also means a job you didn't write is now writing to six ad platforms on a schedule.

Here's how I'd wire it before switching it on:

1. One source of truth for "qualified". If the CRM says customer, no prospecting audience should keep them.
2. Suppression first. Customers, open opportunities, employees, competitors. Then the audience.
3. Log every sync. Rows in, rows out, per platform, per run. If a count jumps, someone should know why.
4. Measure your own match rates. Clay quotes 95% on LinkedIn, 85% on Google and 70% on Meta. Those are vendor figures. Check yours before a spend decision rests on them.
5. Name an owner. An always-on sync with no owner is how ad budget ends up targeting your own customers.

Honest limit: check refresh cadence and pricing on your plan before you rely on it.

Audiences used to be exports. Now they're plumbing. Treat them like it.

Which list would hurt most if it leaked into a prospecting audience?
