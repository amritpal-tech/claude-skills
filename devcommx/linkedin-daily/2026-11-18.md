<!-- post:amritpal format:text ask:sharp-question type:news -->
ChatGPT now links to a website in 47.8% of chats.

That's Peec AI's count for November 14, up from about one in ten in its earlier data.

Peec counts clickable links inside the answer itself. Citations and sidebar links don't count.

The part I'd read twice: 63% of the linked URLs weren't among the sources ChatGPT cited in the same chat. For domains, it was 47%.

So being cited and being linked are two separate wins. A brand can earn one without the other.

Here's how I read the mechanism.

A citation shows where the answer came from. A link shows where ChatGPT sends the reader next.

If your AEO report only tracks citations, it misses the surface where a click can happen.

Here's what I'd check before your next report:

1. Run your top buyer prompts in ChatGPT and log links and citations in separate columns.
2. Note which of your pages get linked: pricing, comparison, signup or docs. Then note which competitor pages get linked instead.
3. Make sure each linked page answers the next step on its first screen.
4. Check your analytics for chatgpt.com referrals and watch the trend over these weeks.

One honest disqualifier: Peec has no traffic data, and its own researcher says more links don't guarantee more clicks.

Cited means you were trusted. Linked means you were useful.

In your own checks, is your brand getting cited, linked, or both?

### Post
<!-- post:pankaj format:text ask:repost-if type:news -->
Your AI agents need a cost field.

At Ignite on November 17, Microsoft put agent spend under management in Agent 365.

Cost Management, in preview, shows IT what drives agent spend and sets policies, limits and alerts. Business Analytics, in Frontier preview, adds spend forecasts and where the spend pays off.

That covers Microsoft's side. A GTM agent's cost is spread over Clay credits, n8n executions, CRM API calls and model tokens. No single bill adds them up.

Without a meter, the first time anyone sees what an agent costs is the invoice. By then it has run for a month.

So I'd meter it myself, starting with the agent that writes the most records:

1. Give every agent an ID, and stamp it on every record it writes.
2. Log each run to one table: agent ID, run ID, credits or tokens used, cost, and the record it touched.
3. Roll it up daily per agent, next to the one outcome that agent exists for: meetings, enriched rows, fixed records.
4. Alert at 80% of the monthly cap. Pause the agent at 100%.
5. Review cost per outcome every month, and retire the agents that don't earn their spend.

The line that matters: an agent you can't price is an agent you can't defend in a budget review.

Plumbing first. Then more agents.

Repost if your agents run on a card nobody checks.
